Baptist

Descriptive: This page keeps General/Free Will, Particular/Reformed, and Southern Baptist diversity distinct rather than treating “Baptist” as one soteriology.

Summary

Baptist history contains multiple soteriological streams. General Baptists developed in an Arminian direction; Particular Baptists adopted Reformed confessions. Modern Baptist bodies inherit, combine, or debate these trajectories. Believer's baptism and congregational patterns do not decide a free-will theory.

Historical streams

Baptist traditions
├── General Baptists
│   ├── John Smyth
│   └── Thomas Helwys
└── Particular Baptists
    ├── Benjamin Keach
    ├── John Gill
    ├── Andrew Fuller
    └── Charles Spurgeon

John Smyth and Thomas Helwys are early General Baptist figures associated with universal provision and responsible response. The 1689 Second London Baptist Confession expresses Particular Baptist Reformed soteriology. Andrew Fuller later modifies strict-hyper-Calvinist tendencies by emphasizing the universal duty to believe while retaining particular redemption.

Grace and conversion

General/Free Will Baptists commonly use Arminian or Wesleyan enabling-grace models. Particular/Reformed Baptists teach effectual calling and monergistic regeneration. Both insist on grace; the disagreement concerns its design and efficacy.

Election and perseverance

Stream Election Grace Security
General/Free Will Baptist Usually conditional Prevenient/enabling, resistible Often conditional
Particular/Reformed Baptist Unconditional Effectual calling Perseverance
Southern Baptist Affirmed, interpretation varies Grace and free agency affirmed BF&M affirms perseverance

The Southern Baptist Convention's Baptist Faith and Message says election is consistent with human free agency and that true believers persevere. It does not resolve every Calvinist/non-Calvinist dispute about effectual calling or the metaphysics of freedom.

Free-will dimensions

No single Baptist answer is accurate. The table keeps the three represented streams separate. “Southern / other” gives the range permitted by the Baptist Faith and Message and common evangelical Baptist usage; it is not a fourth uniform system. See the biblical dossiers.

# Dimension General / Free Will Baptist Particular / Reformed Baptist Southern / other Baptist range Common texts in the dispute
1 Alternative possibilities Grace-enabled alternatives normally required. Not universally required for responsibility. Libertarian and compatibilist accounts coexist. Deuteronomy 30:19; Acts 2:23
2 Sourcehood Enabled response belongs to the agent. Creature is proximate, not ultimate, source. Strong free-agency language; metaphysics unsettled. Matthew 15:18–20; James 1:13–15
3 Voluntariness Grace does not coerce assent. Decree and renewal produce willing, not forced, action. All streams stress personal repentance and faith. Philemon 14; John 6:37
4 Reasons-responsiveness Gospel reasons address enabled hearers. Reasons and preaching are ordained means. Evangelistic appeal is central across streams. Romans 10:14–17; Acts 17:2–4
5 Moral responsibility Universal enabling supports accountable response. Duty, knowledge, and voluntary desire suffice despite inability. Human free agency and accountability strongly affirmed. Luke 12:47–48; Romans 1:18–32
6 Causal determination Rejected for morally free choices. Providential certainty through secondary causes; commonly compatibilist. Calvinist and non-Calvinist models coexist. Proverbs 16:9; 1 Corinthians 10:13
7 Divine determination Providence without exhaustive determination of sin. Comprehensive decree without divine authorship of sin. Strong providence; mode internally debated. Genesis 50:20; Acts 4:27–28
8 Divine foreknowledge Exhaustive; compatible with libertarian choice. Exhaustive and purposive, grounded in decree. Exhaustive classical foreknowledge is typical; models vary. Psalm 139:1–16; Romans 8:29
9 Spiritual ability No unaided saving ability; prevenient grace enables. Total moral inability until regeneration. Inability and need of grace affirmed with differing definitions. John 6:44; Ephesians 2:1–5
10 Grace priority Universal enabling grace precedes faith. Effectual grace supplies the first saving response. Grace precedes salvation; efficacy debated. John 1:9; Acts 16:14
11 Grace efficacy Enables but does not ensure assent. Effectual calling certainly produces willing faith. Calvinist efficacy and universally sufficient enabling both represented. Ezekiel 36:25–27; John 6:37–45
12 Resistibility Saving grace can be finally resisted. General call can be resisted; effectual call succeeds. Internally debated. Acts 7:51; Romans 8:30
13 Regeneration / faith order Enabled faith ordinarily precedes regeneration logically. Regeneration logically precedes and produces faith. Both orders are taught. John 1:12–13; 1 John 5:1
14 Monergism / synergism Synergistic response within prior grace. Monergistic regeneration, followed by active faith. Both, though all reject works-merit. Ephesians 2:4–10; Philippians 2:12–13
15 Election Conditional, corporate or individual in Christ. Unconditional individual election. Election affirmed; Calvinist and non-Calvinist interpretations coexist. Romans 9:6–24; Ephesians 1:3–14
16 Reprobation Unconditional reprobation rejected; condemnation follows unbelief. Asymmetric passing over and judgment for sin. BF&M does not define a theory; many reject reprobation language. Ezekiel 33:11; Romans 9:17–24
17 Perseverance Often conditional security and possible apostasy. Divine preservation through perseverance. BF&M affirms all true believers endure to the end. John 10:27–30; Hebrews 6:4–12

Formative theologians

See Baptist Builders for Smyth, Helwys, Keach, Gill, Fuller, and Spurgeon. These are formative representatives, not founders of one uniform Baptist system.

Common misunderstandings

  • “Baptist” does not entail either Calvinism or Arminianism.
  • Southern Baptist confessional perseverance does not by itself settle every doctrine of election.
  • Strong emphasis on decision is not automatically Pelagian; one must ask what enables the response.
