Wesleyan / Methodist

Descriptive: This page summarizes a mainstream Wesleyan/Methodist account. The section labeled “Reformed Assessment” changes explicitly to a confessional Reformed judgment.

Summary

Wesleyan theology combines total inability apart from grace with universally offered prevenient grace, resistible response, justification by faith, sanctification, and the possibility of apostasy. Enabled choice is a gift, not autonomous natural power.

Human nature and the Fall

John Wesley describes humanity as dead in sin and unable to make the first movement toward God. This is not softened by appeal to a surviving neutral faculty. The reason hearers can respond is that none is left in a state of “mere nature”: preceding grace is already at work.

Grace and conversion

Total inability apart from grace
            ↓
     Prevenient grace
            ↓
Restored/enabled response
       ↙           ↘
    resist        trust
                    ↓
          faith and new birth

The diagram is logical, not a claim about conscious stages. Prevenient grace awakens and enables but does not ensure assent. Conversion is synergistic only after and within divine enabling.

Election and predestination

Wesley rejects unconditional election and reprobation. Election is normally understood as conditional on faith and centered in Christ, with God's universal saving will and foreknowledge affirmed.

Perseverance and apostasy

Believers can finally fall away, though God supplies all grace necessary to persevere. Assurance is a Spirit-given present confidence, not an unconditional guarantee detached from continuing faith.

Sanctification

Wesleyan theology gives unusual prominence to entire sanctification or Christian perfection: perfected love, not sinless omniscience or inability to err. Grace continues to heal desire and empower obedient love after justification.

Free-will dimensions

The table states a mainstream Wesleyan/Methodist pattern; later Holiness, United Methodist, conservative Wesleyan, and other Methodist bodies differ at points. See the biblical dossiers for Reformed and other readings.

# Dimension Wesleyan emphasis Common biblical case
1 Alternative possibilities Prevenient grace restores genuine alternatives for saving response. Deuteronomy 30:15–20; 1 Corinthians 10:13
2 Sourcehood The enabled person owns assent or refusal without originating the grace-enabled capacity. Matthew 23:37; James 1:14–18
3 Voluntariness Grace awakens rather than coerces; faith and holy love are personal responses. Revelation 3:20; 2 Corinthians 9:7
4 Reasons-responsiveness Preaching, warning, and the Spirit-enabled conscience furnish real reasons for repentance. John 16:8–11; Acts 17:30
5 Moral responsibility Universal enabling underwrites accountable response; culpability can vary with light received. John 1:9; Luke 12:47–48
6 Causal determination Commonly rejected for free moral acts; grace influences without necessitating response. Joshua 24:15; 1 Corinthians 10:13
7 Divine determination Strong providence and redemptive purpose without exhaustive determination of sinful choices. Genesis 50:20; Romans 8:28
8 Divine foreknowledge Exhaustive foreknowledge is classically affirmed without making choices causally necessary. Psalm 139:1–16; Romans 8:29
9 Spiritual ability Total inability apart from grace; no surviving neutral natural power initiates salvation. John 6:44; Ephesians 2:1–5
10 Grace priority Universal prevenient grace awakens, convicts, and enables before saving faith. John 1:9; Titus 2:11
11 Grace efficacy Grace genuinely enables and transforms but does not ensure initial assent or final continuance. Titus 2:11–14; 1 Corinthians 15:10
12 Resistibility Yes; persons can reject the Spirit's gracious summons. Matthew 23:37; Acts 7:51
13 Regeneration / faith order Prevenient grace enables faith; justification and new birth answer to faith without being earned by it. John 1:12–13; Galatians 3:26
14 Monergism / synergism Synergistic only within prior grace: God enables, the person responds, and grace remains the source of every good. 2 Corinthians 6:1; Philippians 2:12–13
15 Election Usually conditional on grace-enabled faith, corporate and Christ-centered, never earned. Romans 8:29; Ephesians 1:3–14
16 Reprobation Unconditional reprobation is rejected; condemnation follows freely persisted unbelief against universal grace. Ezekiel 33:11; 1 Timothy 2:4
17 Perseverance Grace is sufficient to preserve, but believers can finally abandon faith. John 15:1–10; Hebrews 6:4–12

Approximate modern mapping: Mainstream Wesleyan accounts are libertarian-leaning because prevenient grace restores alternatives; Wesley's pastoral theology should not be reduced to a later analytic mechanism.

Common misunderstandings

  • Prevenient grace is not Pelagian natural ability.
  • “Free for all” grace does not mean salvation is merited by decision.
  • Christian perfection concerns mature love, not absolute faultlessness.

Reformed Assessment

Wesleyan theology's strongest contributions are its insistence on prior grace, universal gospel invitation, meaningful response, assurance, and holy love. From a confessional Reformed perspective, universal prevenient grace bears a substantial exegetical burden, the decisive-difference problem remains after equal enablement, and possible apostasy gives assurance a different ground from divine preservation. See Why the Wesleyan Solution Is Weaker and the seventeen-dimension assessment.
