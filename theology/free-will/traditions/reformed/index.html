Reformed / Presbyterian

Descriptive: The profile through “Common Misunderstandings” summarizes confessional Reformed teaching. The “Reformed Self-Assessment” section then argues from within that framework.

Summary

Confessional Reformed theology teaches voluntary creaturely action under comprehensive providence, total inability in saving matters, unconditional election, effectual calling, justification by faith, and God's preservation of the saints. Later Reformed accounts are commonly compatibilist, though the confessions do not endorse one modern analytic theory.

Human nature and the Fall

The will retains “natural liberty” and is not forced by an absolute necessity of nature. After the Fall, however, a person cannot convert or prepare themselves for spiritual good accompanying salvation. This is inability of disposition and spiritual condition, not absence of deliberation.

Grace and conversion

The outward gospel call addresses many and can be rejected. In effectual calling God enlightens the mind, renews the will, and draws the elect so that they come willingly. Regeneration is commonly treated as logically prior to faith; faith is the response produced by renewing grace.

Election and predestination

Election is unconditional and not grounded in foreseen faith. Reprobation is usually formulated asymmetrically: Westminster speaks of passing by and ordaining to judgment for sin, while Reformed theologians differ on supra-/infralapsarian ordering and the mode of divine causality. This is not a symmetrical claim that God produces faith and unbelief in the same moral way.

Perseverance

Those truly united to Christ are preserved and persevere. They can fall into serious sin and lose assurance for a time, but not totally and finally lose saving grace. The Canons of Dort ground perseverance in divine mercy and use of means.

Free-will dimensions

This table states the confessional family position compactly. The dedicated seventeen-dimension assessment supplies competing readings, objections, responses, and remaining difficulties; the biblical dossiers give fuller contexts.

# Dimension Confessional Reformed answer Principal biblical case
1 Alternative possibilities Not universally required for responsibility; live deliberation and conditional powers remain. Acts 2:23; Matthew 26:24
2 Sourcehood Creatures are real proximate sources through heart, intention, and reasons, though not ultimate self-creators. Genesis 50:20; James 1:13–15
3 Voluntariness Providence and effectual grace work through, not against, the will; renewal produces willing faith. Psalm 110:3; John 6:37
4 Reasons-responsiveness Rational responsiveness is real and divinely ordained; saving nonresponse flows from corrupt desire. Isaiah 1:16–20; Acts 17:2–4
5 Moral responsibility Grounded in divine norm, knowledge, and voluntary heart-action rather than ultimate sourcehood or unaided saving ability. Luke 12:47–48; Romans 1:18–32
6 Causal determination Not modern physical determinism; all events are certain in providence through causes acting according to their kinds. Proverbs 16:1, 9, 33; Isaiah 10:5–15
7 Divine determination Comprehensive holy decree without coercion or divine authorship of sin; primary and secondary causes differ. Isaiah 46:9–11; Acts 4:27–28
8 Divine foreknowledge Exhaustive, purposive knowledge grounded in God and his decree; “foreknow” can signify fore-love. Psalm 139:1–16; Romans 8:29–30
9 Spiritual ability Natural faculties remain; fallen people lack moral and spiritual ability to turn savingly. John 6:44, 65; Romans 8:7–8
10 Grace priority Grace supplies the first saving disposition and every response, not only opportunity. Acts 16:14; Ephesians 2:1–10
11 Grace efficacy Effectual calling renews the elect so that they certainly and willingly come. Ezekiel 36:25–27; John 6:37–45
12 Resistibility General call and common operations can be resisted; effectual regeneration succeeds by changing the heart. Acts 7:51; Romans 8:30
13 Regeneration / faith order Regeneration is logically prior to faith, though normally inseparable in experience; the renewed person believes. John 1:12–13; 1 John 5:1
14 Monergism / synergism Monergistic regeneration; active faith, repentance, and dependent cooperation in sanctification follow. John 3:3–8; Philippians 2:12–13
15 Election Unconditional individual election in Christ; faith and holiness are results, not foreseen grounds. Romans 9:6–24; Ephesians 1:3–14
16 Reprobation Asymmetric passing over and just condemnation for sin; Reformed orders and causal explanations vary. Romans 9:17–24; Romans 11:7–10
17 Perseverance God preserves the regenerate through persevering faith and ordained means; serious falls are possible, final loss is not. John 10:27–30; Romans 8:28–39

Formative theologians and diversity

Calvin, Theodore Beza, Francis Turretin, and Jonathan Edwards are important but not interchangeable. Reformed Baptist, Presbyterian, Continental Reformed, and Anglican Reformed traditions share family resemblances while differing on covenants, church, and sacraments.

Common misunderstandings

  • Reformed theology does not normally say people are robots; it insists that choices are voluntary and morally significant.
  • “Irresistible grace” names successful renewal of the will, not belief by external compulsion.
  • Predestination is not fatalism: preaching, deliberation, warning, and faith are ordained means.

Reformed Self-Assessment

This self-assessment states the confessional case rather than presenting it as neutral description.

Principal strengths

  • comprehensive providence with real secondary causes;
  • decisive grace and monergistic regeneration;
  • unconditional election and effectual calling;
  • preservation and perseverance;
  • compatibilist voluntary responsibility.

Principal objections requiring defense

  • the author-of-sin problem and the wider problem of evil;
  • moral responsibility under determination;
  • universal-saving-will passages;
  • ultimate-sourcehood, manipulation, and transformed-desire objections.

The dedicated Reformed evaluation presents the cumulative case and comparative costs; the seventeen-dimension assessment gives the biblical case, strongest alternative, exegetical hinge, objection, response, and residual difficulty for every row above.

  • Canons of Dort

  • Edwards, Freedom of the Will
