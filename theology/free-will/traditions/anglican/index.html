Anglican

Descriptive: This page summarizes the classical formularies and the range of Anglican streams without selecting one as normative for the whole Communion.

Summary

Anglicanism has no single founder or single free-will theory. Its classical formularies deny unaided saving ability and affirm preventing and cooperating grace, but Reformed, Arminian, High Church, Wesleyan, and Anglo-Catholic streams develop those commitments differently.

Human nature and the Fall

Article IX treats original sin as corruption affecting every person. Article X says fallen people cannot turn and prepare themselves for faith by natural strength and good works. This distinguishes ordinary responsibility from saving ability.

Grace and conversion

Article X speaks of grace “preventing us, that we may have a good will,” and “working with us, when we have that good will.” Reformed Anglicans read conversion monergistically; Arminian and later Anglo-Catholic streams emphasize enabled cooperation. The Book of Common Prayer locates grace within proclamation, baptism, Eucharist, and prayer.

Election and predestination

Article XVII affirms predestination to life in Christ and treats it as comfort while warning against destructive speculation. It does not settle every later dispute over conditionality, reprobation, or perseverance, enabling competing historical receptions.

Perseverance and apostasy

Anglican answers differ. Reformed streams affirm preservation; Arminian, Wesleyan, and Catholic-leaning streams permit final apostasy. No single row should be attributed to the entire Communion.

Free-will dimensions

Article X supplies a common boundary on inability and preventing grace; beyond it, Reformed, Arminian, High Church, Wesleyan, and Anglo-Catholic answers differ. “Varies” below names a real stream-level disagreement, not a missing answer. See the biblical dossiers.

# Dimension Anglican range Representative biblical case
1 Alternative possibilities Reformed streams deny a universal requirement; Arminian/Wesleyan streams affirm grace-enabled alternatives. Deuteronomy 30:19; Acts 2:23
2 Sourcehood All streams affirm owned voluntary action; ultimate-source requirements vary with metaphysics. Matthew 15:18–20; James 1:13–15
3 Voluntariness Classical liturgy and moral theology require unforced response; accounts of divine motion vary. Philemon 14; Philippians 2:13
4 Reasons-responsiveness Reason is real but fallen; Word, sacrament, prayer, and exhortation are means through which grace addresses persons. Romans 10:14–17; Romans 12:1–2
5 Moral responsibility Sinful corruption does not erase responsibility; knowledge and willing remain relevant. Luke 12:47–48; Romans 1:18–32
6 Causal determination Reformed compatibilist, Thomist-like, and libertarian streams coexist; the Articles do not settle a modern theory. Proverbs 16:9; 1 Corinthians 10:13
7 Divine determination Providence is affirmed; its mode ranges from Reformed decree to permissive or synergistic accounts. Genesis 50:20; Romans 8:28
8 Divine foreknowledge Classically exhaustive; relation to freedom ranges across Reformed, Arminian, and catholic models. Psalm 139:1–16; 1 Peter 1:1–2
9 Spiritual ability Article X denies that fallen people can turn or prepare themselves for faith by natural strength. John 6:44; Ephesians 2:1–5
10 Grace priority Article X's preventing grace gives the good will before working with the renewed will. John 15:5; Philippians 2:13
11 Grace efficacy Reformed effectual calling, Arminian enabling, and sacramental-healing accounts all occur. Ezekiel 36:25–27; Titus 3:4–7
12 Resistibility Reformed streams distinguish effectual grace; Arminian, Wesleyan, and Anglo-Catholic streams ordinarily affirm final resistance. Matthew 23:37; Acts 7:51
13 Regeneration / faith order Reformed logical priority, evangelical conversion idioms, and baptismal-regeneration accounts coexist. John 3:5–8; Acts 2:38
14 Monergism / synergism Article X can be received monergistically for conversion or as prior grace enabling cooperation; later sanctification is active. Ephesians 2:4–10; Philippians 2:12–13
15 Election Article XVII affirms predestination to life in Christ; Reformed unconditional and Arminian conditional readings coexist. Romans 8:28–30; Ephesians 1:3–14
16 Reprobation Article XVII does not define a doctrine; Reformed passing-over and anti-reprobation streams coexist. Romans 9:17–24; 1 Timothy 2:4
17 Perseverance Reformed streams affirm preservation; Arminian, Wesleyan, and Catholic-leaning streams permit final apostasy. John 10:27–30; Hebrews 3:12–14

Formative theologians

Thomas Cranmer and Richard Hooker are formative, not sole founders. Cranmer shaped liturgy and Articles; Hooker defended the Elizabethan settlement and a theological account of law, reason, church, and grace.

Common misunderstandings

  • Henry VIII's political role does not make him the single theological founder of Anglicanism.
  • Article X does not teach Pelagian natural ability.
  • Anglican diversity is not proof that the formularies say nothing; it shows that their boundaries permit different systems.

Authoritative and primary sources

  • Church of England, Articles of Religion, especially IX, X, XVII
  • Book of Common Prayer
  • Richard Hooker, Of the Laws of Ecclesiastical Polity
