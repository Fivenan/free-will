Anglican Builders

Anglicanism emerged through crown, Parliament, bishops, liturgy, inherited catholic structures, and continental reform. Thomas Cranmer and Richard Hooker are formative, not solitary founders.

Thomas Cranmer

Dates: 1489–1556
Role: Archbishop of Canterbury, English reformer, liturgical and confessional architect
Works: 1549 and 1552 Books of Common Prayer; homilies; contribution to the Forty-Two Articles

Cranmer's mature theology stresses fallen inability, justification by faith, and divine initiative. The liturgy makes grace ecclesial and sacramental rather than an abstract decision mechanism. Article X's language of preventing and cooperating grace descends through this Reformation setting, though the final Thirty-Nine Articles postdate his death.

Richard Hooker

Dates: 1554–1600
Role: Anglican priest and formative defender of the Elizabethan settlement
Work: Of the Laws of Ecclesiastical Polity

Hooker situates voluntary action within divine eternal law, created rational participation, church, sacrament, and grace. Reason is not an autonomous rival to Scripture; nature and grace have ordered relations. His soteriology shares Reformation commitments while his account of law and sacrament helps later Anglicans articulate a distinctively broad synthesis.

Later diversity

Reformed Anglicans, Laudian High Church thinkers, Arminian streams, evangelical revivalists, Wesleyans, and Anglo-Catholics develop different models of election, efficacy, and perseverance. Neither Cranmer nor Hooker exhausts them.
