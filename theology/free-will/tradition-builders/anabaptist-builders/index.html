Anabaptist Builders

Anabaptism includes Swiss Brethren, South German/Austrian, Hutterite, Mennonite, and other networks. Strong common themes—believer's baptism, discipleship, disciplined church, and nonconformity—do not erase theological differences.

Menno Simons

Dates: 1496–1561
Role: Dutch former priest and major Mennonite consolidator
Works: Foundation of Christian Doctrine; The New Birth

Menno organizes scattered communities after the Münster crisis and centers true faith on new birth, obedience, peace, and a visible holy life. Grace in Christ is indispensable; the human response is voluntary and embodied in discipleship. Calling this “Pelagian” would ignore his dependence on divine mercy and regeneration.

Conrad Grebel and Felix Manz

Grebel (c. 1498–1526) and Manz (c. 1498–1527) were early Swiss Brethren leaders in the break with Zwingli over baptism and church discipline. Surviving sources focus more on church and obedience than a systematic free-will metaphysics.

Balthasar Hubmaier

Dates: c. 1480–1528
Hubmaier, a learned South German Anabaptist, addresses grace and freedom more explicitly, including On Free Will. He distinguishes the damage of the Fall and restoration through Christ, while defending responsible response. His acceptance of magisterial coercion in some contexts and other differences make him unrepresentative of all Anabaptism.
