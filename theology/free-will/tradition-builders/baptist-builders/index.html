Baptist Builders

Baptist origins include distinct General and Particular streams. The figures below are early leaders and major representatives, not founders of a single uniform doctrine.

General Baptist stream

John Smyth

Dates: c. 1570–1612
Smyth moved through Puritan Separatism, believers' baptism, and contact with Dutch Mennonites. His developing theology opposed strict predestinarianism and helped open a General Baptist trajectory. Because his positions changed rapidly, no single late statement should define all later Baptists.

Thomas Helwys

Dates: c. 1575–c. 1616
Helwys helped establish an English General Baptist congregation and defended religious liberty in A Short Declaration of the Mystery of Iniquity. His soteriology stresses universal provision and responsible response. Liberty of conscience is historically important but is not itself proof of a specific metaphysics of free will.

Particular / Reformed Baptist stream

Benjamin Keach

Dates: 1640–1704
Keach was a major Particular Baptist pastor associated with the 1689 confession. He combines Reformed effectual grace and perseverance with evangelistic proclamation.

John Gill

Dates: 1697–1771
Gill systematizes high Calvinist Baptist theology. His precise relation to later hyper-Calvinism is debated; he should not stand for every Particular Baptist.

Andrew Fuller

Dates: 1754–1815
In The Gospel Worthy of All Acceptation, Fuller distinguishes natural and moral inability and defends the duty of every hearer to believe. He receives Edwards while energizing missionary Particular Baptist life.

Charles Spurgeon

Dates: 1834–1892
Spurgeon combines robust Reformed election and effectual grace with indiscriminate gospel invitation. Popular appeals should be read within, not against, his confessional Calvinism.
