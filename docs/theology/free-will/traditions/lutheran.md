# Lutheran

> **Descriptive:** This page summarizes confessional Lutheran teaching. The section labeled “Reformed Assessment” changes explicitly to a confessional Reformed judgment.

## Summary

Confessional Lutheranism distinguishes ordinary civil agency from the fallen person's inability to cause conversion. The Holy Spirit alone creates faith through Word and sacrament; grace is universal and can be resisted, and believers can fall away. This combination differs from both Reformed and Arminian systems.

## Human nature and the Fall

The Augsburg Confession and Formula of Concord teach deep original corruption and inability in “spiritual things.” They do not deny reason, voluntary outward action, or civil righteousness. Formula II explicitly says the controversy is not about choices in temporal life.

## Grace

The Spirit works through the external Word, baptism, absolution, and the Supper. The gospel is genuinely offered; conversion is not produced by an unaided capacity or a small human contribution. God's action changes an unwilling person into a willing believer.

## Conversion

Conversion is monergistic: Scripture attributes its beginning and completion entirely to the Spirit. After conversion, the renewed will cooperates in sanctification, always dependently. Lutheran monergism therefore concerns a defined act, not the denial of all subsequent human action.

## Election and predestination

Formula XI teaches eternal election to salvation in Christ as a source of comfort. It directs readers to Word and sacraments rather than a hidden symmetrical decree and denies that God causes sin or wills damnation. Condemnation is traced to sin, unbelief, and resistance.

## Perseverance and apostasy

Grace can be resisted and faith can be lost. Lutheran teaching therefore combines monergistic conversion with the possibility of apostasy—evidence that “monergism” does not automatically entail Reformed perseverance.

**Approximate modern mapping:** Saving bondage and monergistic conversion have compatibilist-like features, but universal resistible grace and possible apostasy prevent a simple Reformed-compatibilist classification.

## Formative theologians and diversity

[Martin Luther](../tradition-builders/luther.md) frames the Erasmus dispute; Philip Melanchthon shapes the Augsburg Confession and changes some later formulations; Martin Chemnitz is central to confessional consolidation. Confessional Lutheran churches also differ from liberal and other Lutheran bodies in the contemporary authority assigned to the Book of Concord.

## Common misunderstandings

- Luther did not claim that humans lack wills or make no ordinary choices.
- Lutherans are not “Calvinists with sacraments”: universal grace, means of grace, election, resistance, and apostasy differ.
- Monergistic conversion does not make preaching and sacrament dispensable; they are the Spirit's instruments.

## Reformed Assessment

Confessional Lutheranism is one of the closest alternatives because it strongly preserves bondage of the fallen will, monergistic conversion, and the external means of grace. From a confessional Reformed perspective, the conjunction of monergistic conversion, resistible universal grace, possible apostasy, and an intentionally non-symmetrical account of election leaves a tension that effectual calling and perseverance resolve more systematically. See [Why Lutheranism Comes Close but Remains Weaker](../reformed-evaluation.md#why-lutheranism-comes-close-but-remains-weaker) and the [seventeen-dimension assessment](../reformed-dimensions-biblical-case.md).
