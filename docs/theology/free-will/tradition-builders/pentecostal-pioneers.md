# Pentecostal Pioneers

Pentecostalism has multiple origins in Holiness, healing, revival, Black church, missionary, and global indigenous networks. Charles Fox Parham and William J. Seymour are important pioneers, not a complete founder story.

## Charles Fox Parham

**Dates:** 1873–1929  
**Role:** Holiness evangelist and early Pentecostal pioneer

At Bethel Bible School in Topeka, Parham's circle connected tongues with Spirit baptism and helped formulate “initial evidence.” His apostolic-faith networks influenced early Pentecostal teaching. His racism and later contested reputation require explicit acknowledgment; historical importance is not moral endorsement.

## William J. Seymour

**Dates:** 1870–1922  
**Role:** African American Holiness preacher and leader of the Azusa Street revival

Seymour carried a Holiness-Pentecostal message to Los Angeles in 1906. The Apostolic Faith Mission's interracial worship, testimony, missionary traffic, and periodical made Azusa Street a global catalyst. Seymour emphasized repentance, justification, sanctification, Spirit baptism, divine healing, and expectant response to grace.

Parham influenced Seymour, but Seymour transformed the message in a community with distinct racial, ecclesial, and missionary significance. Naming Parham alone as founder erases this development and the many independent global receptions.

## From Wesley to Pentecostal streams

```text
Wesleyan theology
      ↓
Holiness movements
      ├── Parham/Topeka networks
      ├── Seymour/Azusa Street
      └── other independent revivals
                  ↓
        diverse Pentecostal traditions
```

The diagram is schematic: Finished Work Pentecostals modify the Wesleyan sequence, Oneness movements reshape doctrine, and many global movements arise through local agency.
