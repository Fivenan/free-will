# Tradition Builders and Formative Theologians

This section concerns later theologians and movement leaders, not “Church Fathers.” Labels distinguish antecedents, reformers, confessional architects, major representatives, and movement pioneers. No page treats Catholicism, Orthodoxy, Anglicanism, Baptist history, or Pentecostalism as the creation of one founder.

| Figure | Dates | Tradition/stream | Historical role | Freedom/grace orientation | Legacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Augustine | 354–430 | Latin patristic | Major Western antecedent | Fallen will liberated by efficacious grace | Medieval, Catholic, Lutheran, Reformed debates |
| Thomas Aquinas | c.1225–1274 | Latin Catholic/Scholastic | Formative theologian | Divine motion and real secondary causation | Thomism and later grace controversies |
| Martin Luther | 1483–1546 | Lutheran | Reformer | Monergistic conversion; bondage in saving matters | Lutheran Reformation |
| Philip Melanchthon | 1497–1560 | Lutheran | Confessional architect | Language develops across career | Augsburg Confession and Lutheran systems |
| John Calvin | 1509–1564 | Reformed | Reformer and theologian | Voluntary agency, effectual grace | Reformed confessional development |
| Theodore Beza | 1519–1605 | Reformed | Later systematizer | Strong predestinarian ordering | Reformed orthodoxy |
| Luis de Molina | 1535–1600 | Catholic/Molinist | Formative school theologian | Middle knowledge and libertarian freedom | *De auxiliis* controversy, Molinism |
| Domingo Báñez | 1528–1604 | Catholic/Thomist | Major Thomist representative | Intrinsically efficacious divine motion | Bañezian school |
| Jacobus Arminius | 1560–1609 | Arminian | Formative theologian | Necessary enabling grace, resistible response | Remonstrant and evangelical Arminian traditions |
| Thomas Cranmer | 1489–1556 | Anglican | Reformer/liturgical architect | Fallen inability and preventing grace | Prayer Book and Articles trajectory |
| Richard Hooker | 1554–1600 | Anglican | Formative theologian | Grace perfects nature within law and church | Anglican theological method |
| Menno Simons | 1496–1561 | Mennonite | Consolidator | Grace-enabled discipleship and response | Mennonite identity |
| John Smyth | c.1570–1612 | General Baptist | Early Baptist leader | Arminian-leaning development | General Baptist stream |
| Thomas Helwys | c.1575–c.1616 | General Baptist | Co-pioneer | Responsible faith and religious liberty | English Baptist development |
| Jonathan Edwards | 1703–1758 | Reformed | Theologian/philosopher | Moral inability and compatibilist necessity | Later Reformed free-will theory |
| John Wesley | 1703–1791 | Methodist | Movement founder and theologian | Prevenient grace and resistible response | Methodism, Holiness, Pentecostal antecedence |
| Charles F. Parham | 1873–1929 | Early Pentecostal | Movement pioneer, contested | Holiness/Arminian background | Topeka and initial-evidence teaching |
| William J. Seymour | 1870–1922 | Pentecostal | Movement pioneer | Holiness, grace, and response | Azusa Street and global Pentecostal expansion |
