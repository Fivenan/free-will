# Common Confusions About Free Will

These corrections identify distinctions, not winners.

## If God knows it, God caused it

Knowledge and causation are different relations. The philosophical problem is whether infallibility plus the fixity of the past makes alternatives impossible, not whether a belief mechanically produces an act. See [Foreknowledge and Free Will](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/free-will-foreknowledge/).

## If an act is undetermined, it must be random

Indeterminism does not entail purposelessness, but neither does it by itself supply control. Agent-causal, event-causal, and non-causal libertarians offer different accounts of ownership. See [Libertarian Free Will](philosophical-views.md#libertarian-free-will).

## Compatibilists do not believe in free will

Compatibilists affirm free will while denying that determinism excludes it. Their critics dispute whether voluntariness, ownership, or reasons-responsiveness is enough. See the [SEP entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/compatibilism/).

## Calvinists think people are robots

Reformed confessions affirm voluntary creaturely agency and describe effectual calling as renewing the will so that a person comes willingly. The dispute is whether this account provides the right control, not whether persons literally lack wills. See [Westminster IX–X](https://opc.org/wcf.html).

## Arminians think humans save themselves

Arminius says free will cannot begin or perfect true spiritual good without grace; grace precedes, accompanies, and sustains response. See his [“Grace and Free Will”](https://ccel.org/ccel/arminius/works2/works2.ix.vi.html).

## Synergism means salvation is half God and half human

Synergy means real cooperation made possible and sustained by indispensable grace. It is not a percentage split or an assertion of equal independent causes. See [Grace and Salvation](grace-and-salvation.md#synergism).

## Lutherans are Calvinists with sacraments

Both traditions teach monergistic conversion, but differ on universal grace, means of grace, election, resistance, baptism, and apostasy. Compare [Lutheran](traditions/lutheran.md) and [Reformed](traditions/reformed.md).

## Catholicism has one theory of free will

Catholic doctrine sets boundaries while permitting Thomist/Bañezian, Molinist, and other accounts of efficacy and creaturely causation. See [Roman Catholic](traditions/catholic.md#internal-schools).

## The Church Fathers were all Arminian

Arminianism belongs to a post-Reformation controversy. Many Fathers are anti-fatalist or synergistic, but they address different questions in different vocabularies. See [Church Fathers](church-fathers/index.md).

## Augustine was simply Calvin before Calvin

Calvin receives Augustine within a different scriptural, medieval, ecclesial, and Reformation setting. Augustine's development and sacramental/catholic context cannot be erased. See [Augustine's legacy](church-fathers/augustine.md#augustines-legacy).

## Predestination and fatalism are identical

Fatalism says the outcome occurs regardless of deliberation and means. Predestinarian traditions ordinarily treat preaching, warning, faith, and action as means within providence. See [Predestination](foreknowledge-and-predestination.md#predestination).

## Foreknowledge and predestination are identical

Foreknowledge concerns divine knowledge; predestination concerns divine purpose. Arminian, Molinist, Lutheran, Reformed, Catholic, and Orthodox accounts relate them differently. See [Foreknowledge and Predestination](foreknowledge-and-predestination.md).

## Determinism is fatalism

Determinism says the past and laws fix the future. Fatalism says an outcome obtains regardless of what one does. A compatibilist treats deliberation and action as causally relevant parts of how the determined result occurs.
