# Dimensions of Free Will

Free will is better treated as a multidimensional space than as a single yes-or-no property. The dimensions below include philosophical questions about agency and theological questions about grace. They should be compared, not collapsed.

| Dimension | Core question |
|---|---|
| Alternative possibilities | Could I genuinely have chosen otherwise? |
| Sourcehood | Does the action originate appropriately from me? |
| Voluntariness | Am I acting according to my will rather than under relevant coercion? |
| Reasons-responsiveness | Can my decision-making respond appropriately to reasons? |
| Moral responsibility | Can I appropriately be praised or blamed? |
| Causal determination | Can a causally determined action still be free? |
| Divine determination | Can God ordain an event while human action remains voluntary? |
| Divine foreknowledge | Does infallible foreknowledge make the future necessary? |
| Spiritual ability | Can fallen humans turn toward God unaided? |
| Grace priority | Must grace act before saving faith? |
| Grace efficacy | What does grace actually accomplish in the will? |
| Resistibility | Can grace be resisted? |
| Regeneration/faith order | Does regeneration logically precede faith, follow it, or resist simple sequencing? |
| Monergism/synergism | Who acts in conversion, and in what sense? |
| Election | Is election conditional, unconditional, corporate, or understood otherwise? |
| Reprobation | What is the status of the non-elect, if the system uses this category? |
| Perseverance | Can a true believer finally apostatize? |

> **Interpretive warning**
>
> Two theologians can both affirm “free will” while disagreeing on nearly every other dimension in this table.

## Dimensions of agency

### Alternative possibilities

This is *leeway*: at the relevant moment, was more than one course of action genuinely open? Many libertarian accounts require robust alternatives. Some compatibilists analyze “could have done otherwise” conditionally; other theories deny that alternatives are necessary for responsibility. [Frankfurt-style cases](moral-responsibility.md#frankfurt-style-cases) are designed to separate responsibility from alternatives.

### Sourcehood

Sourcehood asks whether the action issues from the agent in the right way. A person might lack alternatives yet still act from their own values; conversely, an indeterministic event might offer alternatives without being sufficiently controlled by the agent. This is why sourcehood and alternatives are distinct.

### Voluntariness

An action is voluntary when it proceeds through the person's will rather than through the relevant kind of external force. Voluntariness alone may be too weak: manipulation can produce willing action while raising doubts about ownership of the motives.

### Reasons-responsiveness

A reasons-responsive mechanism can recognize relevant reasons and modify action across an appropriate range of possible situations. This idea is central to influential compatibilist accounts, but theories disagree about how much responsiveness and ownership are required.

### Moral responsibility

Responsibility may mean basic-desert praise or blame, accountability within relationships, or forward-looking practices such as protection and moral formation. Conclusions about free will can change with the kind of responsibility at issue. See [Moral Responsibility](moral-responsibility.md).

## Dimensions of determination and knowledge

### Causal determination

Causal determinism says that the total past together with the laws of nature fixes one future. Compatibilists say this can coexist with the relevant freedom; incompatibilists deny it. Determinism is not [fatalism](common-confusions.md#determinism-is-fatalism): on a deterministic account, deliberation may itself help cause the outcome.

### Divine determination

Theological systems differ over how divine willing, primary causation, concurrence, permission, and creaturely secondary causes relate. Divine determination is neither automatically identical to physical determinism nor automatically coercive.

### Divine foreknowledge

Infallible knowledge appears to fix what will happen, but knowledge is not itself causation. The main problem concerns necessity, the fixity of the past, and the dependence between a true future act and divine knowledge. See [Foreknowledge and Predestination](foreknowledge-and-predestination.md).

## Dimensions of sin and grace

### Spiritual ability

This asks a narrower question than ordinary agency: can a fallen person, without grace, initiate a saving response to God? Catholic, Orthodox, Lutheran, Reformed, Arminian, Wesleyan, Anglican, Baptist, and Pentecostal sources considered in this wiki all give grace an indispensable role, while disagreeing sharply about what follows.

### Grace priority

“Prevenient” literally means preceding. Traditions may affirm that grace precedes faith while differing about whether it is sacramentally mediated, universally enabling, effectual for the elect, or described through another framework.

### Grace efficacy

Grace may be described as healing and elevating nature, illuminating and enabling a response, renewing desire, effectually calling, regenerating, or divinizing. The same word *grace* therefore cannot be compared without examining its claimed effect.

### Resistibility

One must specify *which* grace. Reformed theology distinguishes a general gospel call from effectual calling. Lutheran sources affirm monergistic conversion yet also speak of resistance and apostasy. Catholic theology distinguishes sufficient and efficacious grace, with internal schools explaining their relation differently.

### Regeneration and faith order

Some Reformed accounts place regeneration logically before faith; many Arminian and Wesleyan accounts describe prevenient grace before faith and regeneration in connection with faith. Baptismal and sacramental frameworks complicate a simple temporal sequence. “Logical priority” should not be mistaken for a measurable interval.

### Monergism and synergism

[Monergism](grace-and-salvation.md#monergism) assigns the regenerating or converting act to God alone in the relevant sense. [Synergism](grace-and-salvation.md#synergism) affirms indispensable prior grace and real human cooperation. This axis does not settle whether ordinary action is libertarian or compatibilist.

### Election and reprobation

Election may be understood as unconditional, conditional on foreknown faith, corporate and Christocentric, sacramental/ecclesial, or in combinations. Reprobation may mean a positive decree, a passing over, permission, or simply the condemned state of persistent unbelief; these are not equivalent.

### Perseverance

Theories range from guaranteed preservation of all the regenerate, through confidence conditioned on continuing faith, to sacramental accounts in which grace can be forfeited and restored. See [Perseverance and Apostasy](grace-and-salvation.md#perseverance-and-apostasy).
