# Historical Development

The history is neither a steady march toward a modern position nor a single family tree. Questions, vocabularies, and institutions change. The diagrams show influence and controversy, not doctrinal identity.

## Chronological overview

| Period | Representative figures/debates | Change in the question |
|---|---|---|
| Second–third centuries | Justin, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Clement, Origen | Fate, Gnosticism, responsibility, moral formation |
| Fourth century | Athanasius, Cappadocians, Chrysostom | Created agency, healing, ascetic cooperation, incarnation |
| Late fourth–fifth centuries | Augustine, Pelagius, Julian, Cassian | Original sin, interior grace, beginning of faith, predestination |
| Sixth–eighth centuries | Orange, Maximus, John of Damascus | Priority of grace in West; Christological willing and providence in East |
| Medieval West | Anselm, Aquinas, Scotus, Ockham | Intellect/will, first and secondary causes, foreknowledge, merit |
| Reformation | Erasmus/Luther, Calvin, Melanchthon | Bondage, justification, effectual conversion, election |
| Catholic grace controversy | Báñez, Molina, *de auxiliis* | Premotion, middle knowledge, sufficient/efficacious grace |
| Arminian debate | Arminius, Remonstrants, Dort | Conditional election, resistibility, perseverance |
| Eighteenth century | Edwards, Wesley | Moral inability/compatibilism; prevenient grace/sanctification |
| Modern philosophy | Frankfurt, Fischer, Kane, Pereboom and others | PAP, sourcehood, reasons-responsiveness, manipulation, skepticism |

The lines represent reception and argument, not simple inheritance. Molina is also formed by Scholastic traditions beyond Aquinas; Arminius emerges within Reformed debates; Wesley remains Anglican; and Pentecostalism has several origins.

## Broad tradition development

```text
EARLY CHRISTIANITY
├── Greek patristic trajectories
│   ├── Cappadocians and Chrysostom
│   ├── Maximus and John of Damascus
│   └── Eastern Orthodox developments
└── Latin patristic trajectories
    ├── Augustine and Cassian
    └── medieval Latin West
        ├── Catholic schools
        │   ├── Thomist/Bañezian
        │   └── Molinist
        └── Reformation
            ├── Lutheran
            ├── Reformed → some Particular Baptists
            ├── Anglican
            ├── Anabaptist → Mennonite trajectories
            └── Arminian → Wesleyan → some Pentecostal streams
```

Baptist and Pentecostal histories have multiple sources; the arrows intentionally say “some.” Catholic and Orthodox continuity cannot be represented adequately as later denominations branching from single medieval founders.

## Major discontinuities

### Fate is not causal determinism

Early apologists usually answer Stoic, astrological, Gnostic, or pagan fatalisms. Their insistence on choice resembles later incompatibilist concerns, but the target is not a Laplacian theory of laws and a fixed physical past.

### Patristic grace is not one technical term

“Grace” can mean creation, Logos, providential help, incarnation, sacrament, interior healing, or eschatological participation. Augustine's anti-Pelagian precision cannot simply be presumed in Origen or Gregory of Nyssa.

### Christology changes the vocabulary of will

Maximus's natural and gnomic willing arise in debate over Christ's full humanity and two wills. This is not merely another answer to “could I have done otherwise?”

### Reformation disputes reorganize inherited material

Luther and Calvin receive Augustine through late-medieval questions of merit, justification, sacrament, and church. Neither is Augustine copied into a new century.

### Modern responsibility becomes partly independent

Frankfurt-style cases, semi-compatibilism, manipulation arguments, and free-will skepticism can be discussed without a theology of conversion. Their conceptual tools may illuminate historical claims, but should not be treated as the historical authors' self-descriptions.
